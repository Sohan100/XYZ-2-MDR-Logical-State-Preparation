# Phenomenological thresholds vs arXiv:2505.03691: the paper's sequential decoder and boundary conventions

Run: cloud session 019t2nNbJZZVZ4vn44taYrzM, 2026-10-09, base `main` at e2db03a. The machine had 2 cores, so every point uses d = 3, 5, 7 with rounds = d. BP and sequential decoders use 10^4 shots per point, correlated matching 2x10^4, and mwpm 5x10^4. The paper used d = 3..15 with 5x10^4 shots per point. Nothing under `src/mdr/ft` was edited.

**Relation to `../RESULTS.md`.** Another session ran the same routine at the same time and pushed `cloud/phen-2505-03691` first. Its results are in the parent folder: d up to 9 for BM and 11 for MWPM, `seq_match` as the paper analogue, and a code-state-start check. This folder is added on top of that branch and changes nothing in it. It adds three things:

1. A faithful re-implementation of the paper's sequential decoder: hard 1D link matching with the p² vs q^r rule, then [[2,1,1]] conditional priors, then matching (`seq_hard`).
2. A circuit with both of the paper's presumed time boundaries: a code-state start **and** a perfect final round of every check, links included.
3. Correlated matching for both codes.

Where the two runs overlap (XYZ² mwpm, bm; XZZX mwpm, bm at d ≤ 7), they agree within about 2σ.

Files:

- `phen_paper.py`: the paper's conventions as a variant. It has `PaperPhenCircuit` and `SequentialHardDecoder` (the paper's sequential decoder, `seq_hard`).
- `run_points.py`: the point runner. It writes resumable CSVs, one row per (code, variant, noise, basis, decoder, d, p).
- `crossings.py`: pairwise crossings with bootstrap error bars.
- `run_all.sh`, `run_ext.sh`, `run_corr.sh`: the exact commands that produced `data/points.csv`. `data/pilot.csv` holds the 2000-shot pilot that placed the windows.
- `crossings_table.md`: the full crossing table, generated by `python crossings.py data/points.csv --boot 1000`.
- The run scripts are meant to be run from the repository root with the venv active.

## 1. Differences between the paper and our setup

| # | Item | Paper (as quoted) | Ours (`FTMDRCircuit(..., final="frame")`, `CompetitorCircuit`) | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Data noise | Pauli noise of total rate p on every data qubit, once per round, with bias eta = p_z/(p_x+p_y) | Same. `PAULI_CHANNEL_1(p_x, p_y, p_z)` before each of the d rounds, with p_x = p_y. | none |
| 2 | Measurement noise | q = p on plaquettes and links | Same. Every check outcome flips with p, including links, hexagons and truncated boundary checks. | none |
| 3 | Start | A code-state memory: every check has a detector from round 1 | Frame (product-state) preparation. Only the S0 products are deterministic in round 1. The gauge checks (odd links, class-A hexagons, side boundary checks) are random in round 1 and have no round-1 detector. | Removes the bottom time boundary of half the links. mwpm only uses S0, so it is unaffected. It matters for decoders that use link information. |
| 4 | End | A perfect final round of all checks (standard; the quote does not say) | A noiseless destructive readout in the frame. It is a perfect last round for S0, but the gauge checks (odd links) get no final value. | Same as row 3, at the top boundary |
| 5 | Logical / failure | Code-level simulation. Presumably a failure is any logical error (not stated in the quote). | One observable per run: X̄ or Ȳ for XYZ², X or Z for XZZX. We report each basis and take the weaker one. | The weaker basis approximates "any logical" asymptotically |
| 6 | Boundaries / code | The open XYZ² patch (2d² qubits, d² XX links, d²-1 plaquettes) | `XYZ2StabilizerGenerator`, the same family. We could not check the paper's boundary layout (no web access). | Assumed identical |
| 7 | Decoder "matching" | Sequential: hard 1D time matching per link (data if p² > q^r), then [[2,1,1]] conditional probabilities, then matching of the 3D YZZY problem with non-iid priors | `mwpm`: static matching on the S0 graph with exact DEM priors. It **uses no link information at all**. `seq_soft`: the soft version (exact link-only marginals; BP on the link chains is a tree), then matching. | Our mwpm is a weaker decoder than the paper's, and seq_soft a stronger one |
| 8 | Decoder "belief-matching" | Sequential hard link decisions, then BM on the higher-level 3D problem | `bm`: BP (10 iterations) on the **full** spacetime DEM (links and plaquettes jointly, soft), with posteriors pushed onto the S0 graph, then matching | **The main source of our higher numbers** |
| 9 | Threshold | Crossing over d = 3..15 | Pairwise crossings p*(3,5) and p*(5,7) with d ≤ 7. The repo's own rule (docs/fair_comparison.md) asks for a finite-size fit over d ≥ 11. | Small-d drift of ±0.3-0.5 % (compare the two columns) |
| 10 | Bias convention | p = total rate, eta = p_z/(p_x+p_y) | Same (`CircuitNoise.phenomenological`) | none |
| 11 | Schedule | n/a | Irrelevant under phen: every check is one noiseless MPP. We used `SCHEDULES["both"]`. | none |

The variant in `phen_paper.py` fixes rows 3, 4 and 7:

- `PaperPhenCircuit` adds a noiseless round 0 after the frame preparation, so the run starts from a code state with every check known. Then come d noisy rounds and a noiseless final round of every check plus the logical (`final="ideal"`). Every link has detectors at both time boundaries.
- `seq_hard` reproduces the paper's sequential decoder. Each link's detection events are matched exactly in 1D: a pair at separation r counts as two data flips if p_f² > q^r, else as r measurement errors. Here p_f = 2a(1-a) is the per-round link-flip probability and a = p_y + p_z. A lone event at the final round counts as a measurement error plus a data flip in the last noisy round. The hard link flags then set the conditional probability of every single-qubit fault in that round (the code-capacity [[2,1,1]] step):
  - flagged: P(Y/Z on one qubit) = p_e/(2a), P(X) = p_x/(2(1-a));
  - unflagged: P(Y/Z) = p_e·a/(1-2a+2a²), P(X) = p_x(1-a)/(1-2a(1-a)).

  A decided link measurement error gets weight 0; an undecided one gets weight 10^-9. These priors go onto the S0 graph, which is matched once over all rounds.
- Two choices are mine because the quote leaves them open. The rule uses p_f rather than the literal p; with q = p this only changes whether pairs at r = 2 count as data. Fault marginals are pushed to edges, as `TwoLevelDecoder` does.
- `two_level()` reuses `TwoLevelDecoder` on this circuit by temporarily rebinding the name `FTMDRCircuit` inside the module, so that its internal S0 circuit has the same structure.

## 3. Verdict

**Why our numbers are above the paper's: mainly the decoder, not the noise model.** The noise model is the same: p on the data, q = p on every check, and the same eta. Our "bm" is not the paper's belief-matching. It runs BP on the full spacetime DEM, with link and plaquette syndromes decoded jointly and softly. The paper decides the links first, with hard time-direction matching, and only then decodes the plaquettes. When we run the paper's sequential decoder on the paper's conventions (`seq_hard`), we get:

| eta | seq_hard (5,7), X | seq_hard (5,7), Y | paper "matching" (d = 3..15) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 3.17 ± 0.09 | 3.23 ± 0.10 | 3.42 |
| 10 | 3.55 ± 0.06 | 3.41 ± 0.13 | 3.49 |

At eta = 10 the reproduction matches. At eta = 0.5 it is about 0.2 % low, which fits small-d drift (rising from (3,5) to (5,7)) and the open p² vs p_f² choice. Making the same link step soft (`seq_soft`) gains 0.2-0.7 %. Joint BP (`bm`) gains about 0.5 % at eta = 0.5 and 1.2-2 % at eta = 10. The paper's own BM (4.30 % at eta = 10) sits between its matching and our bm, as expected for "hard links, then BP on the upper level".

The boundary conventions (rows 3-4) change the small-d crossings by about ±0.3 % (bm: 3.70 → 3.98 at eta = 0.5 X; 5.50 → 5.23 at eta = 10 X). They also lower p_L at fixed p a lot at eta = 10: the d = 3 bm p_L falls by more than half, because every link gets both time boundaries. They do not explain a systematic upward shift. Our static `mwpm` ignores the links entirely and so lies below the paper's matching: 3.05-3.43 vs 3.42, and 3.10-3.28 vs 3.49.

**Which numbers are comparable:**

- Our `seq_hard` (paper variant) ↔ the paper's matching.
- None of ours matches the paper's BM exactly. Our `bm` is a stronger decoder: the paper's BM is a lower bound for it.
- Our `mwpm` is "matching without the sequential link step". It is not the paper's matching.
- The user's pilot numbers line up with our bm: 3.6-4.0 % (our bm X 3.70, Y 4.41) and 5.4-5.7 % (our bm X 5.50, Y 6.00). They are thresholds of a different, stronger decoder than the paper's and are not in conflict with it.
- None of these d ≤ 7 crossings is a threshold under the repo's own rule (finite-size fit over d ≥ 11).

**XYZ² vs XZZX, each with its best decoder, counted in its weaker basis, crossing p*(5,7):**

| eta | XYZ² (ours) | XYZ² (paper conventions) | XZZX | Who leads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 3.85 ± 0.06 (X, HCM; bm 3.70) | 3.98 ± 0.08 (X, bm) | 4.45 ± 0.10 (X, bm; Z bm 4.60) | **XZZX, by about 0.5-0.75 %** (clear) |
| 10 | 5.50 ± 0.11 (X, bm; Y 6.00) | 5.23 ± 0.16 (X, bm) | 5.10 ± 0.21 (Z, bm; mwpm 5.04, corr 4.92; X 5.8-6.0) | **XYZ² by 0.4 % (≈1.7σ) with our circuit; a tie under the paper's conventions** |

So XYZ² does **not** beat XZZX under depolarizing phenomenological noise. At eta = 10 XYZ² is ahead at d ≤ 7, but not significantly and not robustly:

- Under the paper's boundary conventions the gap disappears.
- The XZZX Z-basis crossing drifts upward. Here it goes 4.56 → 5.10. In the parallel run's larger-d data (`../RESULTS.md` §3.2) it reaches 5.23 ± 0.09 (7/9) and 5.34 ± 0.15 (9/11) with mwpm, and 5.37 ± 0.33 (7/9) with bm. Meanwhile XYZ² X-basis bm stays at 5.48 ± 0.20 (7/9).
- XYZ² uses twice as many data qubits per d (2d² vs d²).

Combined with the larger-d data, eta = 10 is a tie. This supports the paper's statement that XYZ² is competitive with codes tailored to biased noise, but it does not establish an advantage. (The paper's XZZX comparison is about code capacity only; see `../RESULTS.md` §1.)
