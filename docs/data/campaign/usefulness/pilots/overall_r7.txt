**Answer.** XYZ^2 has no established use on any modelled hardware, either as a memory or for r = 1 MDR state preparation (C33, C36, C37, C38). The measured result is negative.
- **Memory thresholds.** On every gate-based hardware-motivated model, XYZ^2's threshold is nominally below the best competitor's at every round count with competitor fits. The one exception is a fit artifact, h2_p at r = 5 (ratio 1.41). With stat plus syst errors the threshold is never significantly above the best competitor's (C2, C13, C15, C27, C33, C36). Under native pair measurements (em3), XYZ^2 is 1.29-1.36x the rotated surface codes' cat-state compilations, but the honeycomb's threshold is 6.1x XYZ^2's (C14).
- **Memory at equal qubits.** On the gate models run at equal qubits (SD6, biased10, biased100, purez), XYZ^2 has a higher logical error per round than the best competitor run at every resolved pair (C4, C6, C32). The same holds in the spin readout corner hw_rr with matching-class decoders (C20). The only device-like points where XYZ^2 is lower are the f = 0 spin mixtures of the idealized hw_tr corner. In m1, the maximum-fidelity proxy of the example spin device, XYZ^2 is 0.70(4) of XZZX, a nominal lead that is not established. Run A is a tie (C33, C37, C38).
- **r = 1 preparation.** XYZ^2's MDR-basis threshold loses on 10 of 13 hardware-motivated models, ties on 3 and wins on none (C10). At every fixed footprint and device tested, a surface-code patch that fits prepares the state better than XYZ^2 does in its MDR basis. This includes the 35-qubit SI1000 footprint, where a 6x3 CSS patch is about 18-19x better (C10, C11, C36).
- **Competitive only in two idealized memory models.** In hw_tr (C35) and phen_b10 (C31), XYZ^2 decoded with belief-matching (BM) has a nominally lower memory error per round than square XZZX at equal qubits. Among the qubit-matched pilot leads, only hw_tr at p_T 4% and r = 6d (0.384(39)) passes C33's rule, and it is measured against XZZX only. Every r = 3d lead is delta-dependent or a tie (C31, C33, C35). Both edges need BM or BP decoding (C23). Both reverse for r = 1 preparation, where XYZ^2 is 17-26x worse in hw_tr and 8.4-15.1x worse in phen_b10 (C9, C10, C31, C35).
- **Device-like variants.** No device-like variant of hw_tr establishes a lead. No edge is seen when 2% of the readout dephasing is also applied in every gate layer (f = 0.02). At the example device's surface-code QEC-optimal readout (run E), XYZ^2 is 1.72(16)x worse (C37, C38).

**Conventions.**
- **Ratios.** Threshold ratios are XYZ^2/competitor, so below 1 means XYZ^2 is worse. Equal-qubit error ratios are XYZ^2/competitor, so above 1 means XYZ^2 is worse.
- **Labels.** A loss means XYZ^2 is at least 3 sigma below some competitor. A tie means it is within 3 sigma of every competitor. A win means it is at least 3 sigma above the best competitor (C33).
- **Threshold errors.** Threshold z values use stat plus syst errors (the err column of docs/data/campaign/thresholds.csv) unless marked stat only (C33).
- **Threshold rules.** The table rule is the selection in docs/data/campaign/competitors_table.csv. The symmetric rules restrict XYZ^2 to its both-bases (both) schedule with mwpm, corr_gauge or bm, against competitor mwpm, corr or bm (docs/data/campaign/usefulness_pilots/fairrev/sym.py) (C2, C24).
- **Equal-qubit campaign.** Point brackets are 68% Wilson intervals. Ratio brackets combine the opposite endpoints of the two intervals (docs/data/campaign/eq/) (C6).
- **Pilot statistic.** Pilots are in docs/data/campaign/usefulness_pilots/ and are not campaign standard. A pilot ratio R carries its Poisson error. Its significance is z = |ln R|/sigma, stat only, the same for leads and losses, under three error models (C33):
  - (a) Poisson;
  - (b) Poisson errors scaled by the pool's own replicate scatter f = max(1, sqrt(chi2/dof)), or by f = 1.7 for a single run, and read as Student t when f > 1 is estimated;
  - (c) an extra run-level log-rate offset delta for each XYZ^2 BM run.
  Pilot z values are quoted as (a), (b), (c) at delta 0.15/0.25.
- **Pilot rule.** A pilot lead or loss holds when z is at least 3 under (a) and (b) and stays at least 3 under (c) up to delta = 0.37 (C33).
  - delta lies in about 0.065-0.37. The 0.065 comes from all 42 replicate sets and is a lower estimate. The four sets flagged after inspection give 0.165-0.374.
  - delta* is the largest delta at which (c) still gives 3 sigma.
  - A point that passes (a) and (b) with delta* below 0.37 is delta-dependent and is not counted. A point that fails (a) or (b) is a tie.
  - Pilots without XYZ^2 BM runs fall outside (c) and are labelled on (a) and (b) alone.

**Where the cost comes from.**
- **Threshold gap (docs/data/campaign/gap/fractions.csv; C22).**
  - With noiseless links and no idling, XYZ^2 ties XZZX at a threshold ratio of 1.013(30).
  - Idling accounts for 0.60(1) of the gap. That variant removes gate-layer, reset and readout idling together.
  - Link noise accounts for 0.23(2) against XZZX without idling, or 0.43(4) against XZZX under SD6.
  - The two effects are super-additive (about 3.5 sigma).
  - With noiseless links but idling kept, XYZ^2 reaches 0.77(2) of XZZX (C34).
  - Noise on the weight-6 measurements does not account for the threshold gap to XZZX (C34).
- **Idling.** XYZ^2 has 8 ticks per round against 6.
  - At 99/97 qubits it has 254 gate-layer idle sites per round against 52: 130 on data qubits, 100 on link ancillas that wait through 4 of the 6 gate layers, and 24 on plaquette ancillas. Reset and readout-window sites are 100 against 98 (C25).
  - In a d = 7/9 MWPM schedule-search diagnostic, removing only the gate-layer idling raises XYZ^2's crossing from 0.38(2)% to 0.57(4)%. That is about 0.70(16) of the gain from removing all idling (C22, C34).
  - That the idling cost comes mainly from the deeper gate schedule is an inference from these counts and that diagnostic.
- **Hardware.** At equal distance XYZ^2 needs about twice the qubits, 2.08-2.25x the two-qubit gates per round, coupler degree 6 against 4, and three gate types against two (C25).
- **Below threshold.** At equal qubits XYZ^2 has 1/sqrt2 of the distance (inference; C4).
- **Time boundary.** MDR preparation sits on XYZ^2's weak side. In phen_b10, XYZ^2's r = 1 threshold reaches at most 15.6%, against 23-29% for XZZX (C9).

**Conclusion (inference).** XYZ^2 is not a useful memory or r = 1 preparation code for any modelled hardware (C33, C36, C37, C38).
- It is competitive only as a memory in two idealized models with BM decoding. In hw_tr one point (p_T 4%, r = 6d) is established, against XZZX only. In phen_b10 no lead is established (C31, C33, C35).
- Both idealized edges become order-of-magnitude losses for r = 1 preparation, the protocol the paper studies (C9, C10).
- Among the device-like hw_tr variants, m1 (f = 0) keeps a nominal lead that is not established. No edge is seen at f = 0.02. Run E, at the surface code's QEC-optimal readout, is a loss for run offsets up to 0.22 (C37, C38).
- The square-patch lead in the SI1000 35-48-qubit window disappears against rectangular patches of the same footprint (C11, C36).
- Several conditions for a spin-device use remain untested (C35, C37, C38):
  - f between 0 and 0.02;
  - each code at its own optimal readout time;
  - eta = 10 at p_T 4-6%;
  - a real-time BP-class decoder.
