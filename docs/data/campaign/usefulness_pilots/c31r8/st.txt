Measured in pooled pilots, not campaign standard. The setting is memory at r = 3d. XYZ^2 BM in its weaker basis is compared with square XZZX in its distance-d frame, decoded with corr, which is XZZX's lower-error decoder at d = 4 and 7. Ratios R are XYZ^2/XZZX with Poisson errors. All errors are statistical; no systematic error is included. Significance follows C33 item 4 in log form, z = |ln R|/sigma, under three error models: (a) Poisson; (b) shot-level scaling, with f = max(1, sqrt(chi2/dof)) from a pool's own replicates and f = 1.7 for a single run, on either side, and with z read as Student t on k - 1 dof where f > 1 is estimated from k replicates; (c) an extra log-rate variance delta^2 per XYZ^2 BM run, with competitor runs Poisson. delta* is the largest delta at which (c) still gives z = 3. C33 puts delta at about 0.065-0.37: 0.065 over all 42 replicate sets (0.074 over the 35 XYZ^2 BM sets), a lower estimate, and 0.165-0.374 for the four flagged sets. Labels follow C33 item 4. A lead or loss holds if z is at least 3 under (a) and (b) and delta* is at least 0.37. A point that passes (a) and (b) with delta* between 0.065 and 0.37 is delta-dependent and is not counted. A point that fails (a) or (b) is a tie. Rows give R, z under (a) and (b), and delta*.

Scope: the lead is a memory result. At r = 1 (single-round MDR preparation, C10), p = 3%, 50/49, the archived pilot gives XYZ^2 BM L_X 1.446(86)e-3 (284 errors) against XZZX corr L_X, XZZX's weaker basis, 1.717(52)e-4 (1080 errors). - The lead needs BP-class decoders. bp_full replicates agree with BM. At p = 2%, 50/49, L_X, XYZ^2 corr_gauge is 1.62(3)x XZZX corr and XYZ^2 MWPM is 7.62(8)x XZZX MWPM.
- Noiseless readout (q = 0), p = 3%: 18/16 gives 0.62(4); z 7.5, 4.4; delta* 0.144, a delta-dependent lead. At 50/49 XYZ^2 BM is worse than XZZX corr in both bases. L_Y, the worse basis, is 1.44(9) (four runs, chi2 1.98 on 3); z 6.0, 4.7; delta* 0.213, a delta-dependent loss. L_X is 1.22(8) (chi2 4.73 on 3, f = 1.26); z 3.1, 1.6 (Student t on 3 dof), a tie. BP-class decoders do not bring L_Y below XZZX (bp_corr 1.56(28), bp_full 1.38(17)). A single bp_corr L_X point, 0.67(18), rests on 15 errors.
- Z bias eta = 100, p = 3%, 50/49: 0.98(9). Readout q = p/10, p = 5%, 50/49: 0.92(5).
- More readout noise helped XYZ^2 here. That may run against hw_tr at p_T 1.5% with readout 2.7% (C37), where readout and p_T are confounded.

Competitors and frames:
- The d^2 frame under Z bias was run as the XY code. Square XZZX aligned to Y differs from XY by X<->Y on half the data qubits. That map leaves phen_b10 unchanged (p_X = p_Y, noiseless checks with outcome flips), so the XY runs stand for it (inference from the circuit code). With BM and corr this code loses to both XZZX corr and XYZ^2. At p = 2%, 49 qubits, XY BM is 1.33(11)x XZZX corr, and XYZ^2 L_Y is 0.61(6)x XY BM. XY with corr does worse still. At p = 2.5%, XYZ^2 L_X is 0.59(6)x XY BM.
- At code capacity with eta = 10 and near-ML decoding (EWD for XYZ^2; MPS, the paper's stated XZZX decoder, for XZZX), 2112.06036 (Sec. 4.3, Fig. 7) finds a lower XYZ^2 failure rate than the d-frame XZZX at equal qubits under Z bias (Fig. 7a inset), the direction seen here. Under Y bias, where XZZX is in its d^2 frame, XZZX is suppressed and its failure rate scales with the number of data qubits (Fig. 7c). That means no clear XYZ^2 advantage over the d^2 frame at equal data qubits (inference from the text and figure). The XY ordering here is the reverse, which points to decoder limits rather than the frame (inference). ML-class decoding of the d^2-frame code is untested and could remove this lead.
- Rectangular, co-prime and tailored XZZX were not run.
